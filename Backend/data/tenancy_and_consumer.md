## Security Deposit Basics

**Topic:** Rental security deposit
**Keywords:** security deposit, advance deposit, rental deposit, deposit refund, deposit amount, tenancy deposit
**Law:** State Rent Control Acts; Model Tenancy Act, 2021
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

A security deposit is money the tenant pays upfront to secure performance of
the tenancy — it remains the tenant's money held by the landlord, not the
landlord's income. There is no single national rule: deposit norms come from
the tenancy agreement and the applicable state law or Rent Control Act.

- Practice varies widely by city — one to three months' rent is common in
  many markets, while some southern cities have a convention of much higher
  deposits. The Model Tenancy Act, 2021 caps residential deposits at two
  months' rent, but it applies only where a state has adopted it.
- The deposit must be refunded when the tenancy ends, minus only lawful
  deductions — typically unpaid rent or utility bills and the cost of damage
  beyond normal wear and tear.
- Ordinary wear and tear (minor scuffs, faded paint, reasonable ageing)
  cannot lawfully be charged against the deposit.
- Always get the deposit amount, refund conditions, and deduction rules in
  writing in the agreement, and take dated photos or a video of the premises
  at move-in and move-out.

## Landlord Not Returning the Security Deposit

**Topic:** Deposit not refunded
**Keywords:** deposit not returned, landlord not returning deposit, deposit refund dispute, security deposit withheld, landlord refusing deposit
**Law:** State Rent Control Acts; Model Tenancy Act, 2021; Specific Relief / civil recovery
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

A landlord who withholds a deposit after the tenancy ends without a lawful
deduction is essentially keeping money that belongs to the tenant. The remedy
is a stepwise escalation, not self-help.

- First, make a written request referencing the agreement's refund clause
  and the agreed timeline — a clear paper trail matters later.
- If that fails, send a formal legal notice demanding refund within a stated
  period; a large share of deposit disputes settle at the notice stage.
- If it still fails, the tenant can approach the Rent Authority or file a
  civil suit for recovery of money in the appropriate court, depending on
  the state and the amounts involved. Small Claims / fast-track courts can
  be an option for modest sums.
- Whether a tenant qualifies as a "consumer" for a consumer complaint on a
  deposit dispute is debatable — courts have differed — so the civil/Rent
  Authority route is the more reliable default.
- Preserve the agreement, deposit payment proof, vacating/handover evidence,
  photos of the premises at handover, and all correspondence with the
  landlord.

## Rent Agreement Essentials

**Topic:** Rent agreement / leave and licence
**Keywords:** rent agreement, lease agreement, leave and licence, rental agreement, agreement registration, 11 month agreement, lock-in period
**Law:** Registration Act, 1908; Transfer of Property Act, 1882; Indian Stamp Act (stamp duty varies by state)
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

The rent agreement is the single most important document in a tenancy —
most deposit and eviction disputes are decided by what it says.

- A clear agreement should record the parties, the premises, rent and
  due date, the security deposit and refund terms, the duration, notice
  period for termination, maintenance responsibilities, and any lock-in
  period.
- Leases of immovable property from year to year, or for a term exceeding
  one year, must be registered — this is why the "11-month agreement"
  convention is common: it stays under the compulsory-registration line in
  many states, though stamp duty rules still apply.
- An unregistered agreement that required registration cannot be relied on
  to prove the tenancy terms in court — registration is worth the small
  cost and effort.
- Rent paid in cash above certain monthly amounts attracts income-tax
  TDS obligations for the tenant in some cases; check current thresholds
  if the rent is high.

## Eviction and Unlawful Eviction

**Topic:** Eviction process and tenant protection
**Keywords:** eviction, eviction notice, landlord eviction, thrown out, locked out, forceful eviction, notice to vacate
**Law:** State Rent Control Acts; Model Tenancy Act, 2021; Transfer of Property Act, 1882, Section 106
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

A landlord cannot evict a tenant by force, however strong their grounds are.
Eviction requires due process — valid legal grounds, written notice, and in
most states an order from a Rent Authority, Rent Court, or civil court.

- Common lawful grounds include non-payment of rent, unauthorized
  subletting, misuse or damage to the premises, the landlord's bona fide
  personal need, and expiry or breach of the tenancy terms.
- Changing the locks, cutting off water or electricity, removing the
  tenant's belongings, or physically throwing the tenant out is unlawful —
  even where genuine grounds for eviction exist. A tenant facing this can
  complain to the police and seek urgent court relief to be restored.
- For tenancies governed by the Transfer of Property Act, termination
  generally requires a written notice to quit — 15 days for a
  month-to-month tenancy and six months for a year-to-year lease,
  unless the agreement or state law provides otherwise.
- If you receive an eviction notice, respond in writing within the notice
  period and do not simply vacate or ignore it — either can hurt your case.

## Rent Receipts and Proof of Payment

**Topic:** Rent receipts and records
**Keywords:** rent receipt, rent proof, rent paid cash, no receipt, rent payment record, hra receipt
**Law:** Indian Stamp Act (revenue stamp on receipts); Income-tax rules for HRA
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Proof that rent was actually paid is what protects a tenant in almost every
later dispute — eviction for "non-payment", deposit refund, and income-tax
HRA claims all stand or fall on it.

- Ask for a signed rent receipt for every payment; receipts above the
  prescribed cash amount need a revenue stamp affixed.
- Paying by bank transfer, UPI, or cheque creates an automatic record that
  is far stronger than cash — note "rent for <month>" in the transfer
  remarks.
- If the landlord refuses receipts, bank records, WhatsApp acknowledgements,
  and the agreement itself still help establish payment history.
- Salaried tenants claiming House Rent Allowance must produce rent
  receipts to their employer and quote the landlord's PAN where the annual
  rent crosses the notified threshold.

## Repairs and Maintenance Responsibilities

**Topic:** Repairs and maintenance in a tenancy
**Keywords:** repairs, maintenance, landlord not repairing, leaking roof, plumbing, who pays for repairs, habitability
**Law:** State Rent Control Acts; Model Tenancy Act, 2021
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Responsibility for repairs is split between landlord and tenant by the
agreement and state law — a common default is structural/major repairs on
the landlord and day-to-day upkeep on the tenant.

- The landlord is generally expected to keep the premises habitable:
  structural repairs, major plumbing/electrical faults, waterproofing, and
  essential services.
- The tenant is generally expected to handle routine upkeep and to not
  damage the premises; damage beyond wear and tear can lawfully be
  deducted from the deposit.
- If the landlord refuses essential repairs, give written notice with a
  deadline; some Rent Acts allow the tenant to carry out the repair and
  deduct the cost from rent, but only after following the prescribed
  notice steps — keep estimates and bills.
- Withholding rent entirely without following the statutory procedure is
  risky and can itself become a ground for eviction.

## Who Can File a Consumer Complaint and About What

**Topic:** Consumer rights and who can complain
**Keywords:** consumer complaint, who is a consumer, defective product, deficiency in service, unfair trade practice, misleading advertisement, consumer rights
**Law:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019
**Source:** Department of Consumer Affairs | https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/

A "consumer" under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 is anyone who buys
goods or hires services for consideration — online and offline purchases
both count. The law excludes goods/services bought for commercial resale or
large commercial purposes.

- Complaints can be about defective goods, deficient services (delay,
  negligence, not delivering what was promised), unfair trade practices,
  overcharging above the printed price, unfair contract terms, and
  misleading advertisements.
- Complaints can also be filed by a recognised consumer association,
  multiple consumers with a common interest, or the legal heir of a
  deceased consumer.
- Product liability means a manufacturer, seller, or service provider can
  be made to pay compensation for harm caused by a defective product or
  deficient service — not just replace or refund.
- Ordinary individual-to-individual disputes (for example a private
  landlord-tenant matter) are generally not consumer matters — they belong
  in civil courts or the Rent Authority.

## Where to File a Consumer Complaint

**Topic:** Consumer forum jurisdiction
**Keywords:** district consumer commission, state consumer commission, national consumer commission, ncdrc, consumer forum, pecuniary jurisdiction, where to file
**Law:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction) Rules, 2021
**Source:** National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission | https://ncdrc.nic.in/

Consumer complaints go to a three-tier system of commissions, allocated by
the value of the goods or services paid (not the compensation sought):

- District Commission — up to Rs. 50 lakh, filed where the complainant
  resides or works, or where the opposite party has an office or the cause
  of action arose.
- State Commission — above Rs. 50 lakh and up to Rs. 2 crore; also hears
  appeals against District Commission orders.
- National Commission (NCDRC) — above Rs. 2 crore; also hears appeals
  against State Commission orders.
- Orders can be appealed upward: District to State within 45 days, State
  to National within 30 days, and National to the Supreme Court.

## How to File a Consumer Complaint

**Topic:** Filing procedure for consumer complaints
**Keywords:** file consumer complaint, edaakhil, e-daakhil, consumer complaint online, national consumer helpline, nch 1915, complaint steps, consumer forum documents
**Law:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019; Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020
**Source:** e-Daakhil Portal | https://edaakhil.nic.in/

Before filing, send the seller or service provider a written complaint or
legal notice and give them a reasonable chance to fix the problem — many
disputes resolve here, and the notice becomes evidence if they ignore it.

- File on the e-Daakhil portal (edaakhil.nic.in) — complaints can be filed
  online for any commission without visiting in person; the National
  Consumer Helpline (1915, or consumerhelpline.gov.in) is a free
  pre-complaint grievance channel worth trying first.
- The complaint should state the facts, the defect or deficiency, the
  relief sought (refund, replacement, repair, compensation), and attach
  documents: bills/invoices, warranty cards, correspondence, photos, and
  the legal notice with proof of delivery.
- No lawyer is required — consumers can argue their own case, and the
  procedure is deliberately simpler than a civil suit.
- Court fees are modest and slab-based on the claim value; complaints up
  to Rs. 5 lakh require no fee.
- If the dispute qualifies and both sides agree, the commission can refer
  the matter to mediation instead of a full hearing.

## Reliefs Available and Time Limits

**Topic:** Consumer remedies and limitation
**Keywords:** consumer relief, refund, replacement, compensation, limitation period, two years consumer complaint, punitive damages
**Law:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019
**Source:** Department of Consumer Affairs | https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/

A consumer complaint must generally be filed within two years of the cause
of action — a delay can still be condoned if the complainant shows
sufficient cause, but late filing adds risk.

- Commissions can order removal of the defect, replacement of the goods,
  refund of the price, discontinuation of the unfair practice, and
  compensation for loss or injury — including compensation for mental
  agony in deserving cases.
- Where negligence caused harm, product-liability compensation can be
  awarded against the manufacturer, seller, or service provider.
- An opposite party that ignores a commission's order faces enforcement —
  non-compliance is itself punishable.
- Keep all documents from day one: invoice, warranty, service records,
  correspondence, photos/videos of the defect, and proof of payment — the
  strength of a consumer case is almost entirely in its paper trail.
